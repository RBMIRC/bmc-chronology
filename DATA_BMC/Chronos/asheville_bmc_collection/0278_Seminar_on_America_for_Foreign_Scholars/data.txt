BLACK MOUNTAIN COLLEGE BLACK MOUNTAIN, N.C.
SEMINAR ON AMERICA FOR FOREIGN SCHOLARS, TEACHERS AND ARTISTS SUMMER 1945
July 1 August 12
Director: Professor Herbert A Miller, sociologist, and director for the past three years of the American Seminar sponsored by the American Friends Service Committee.
The Seminar on America is planned to provide an opportunity for qualified newcomers with specialized training to accelerate their adjustment to American community life and to get a better understanding of American democracy and history. Emphasis will be placed upon principles and methods of education. Intensive tutoring in oral and written English will be offered.
The seminar should especially interest persons who need to improve their English and general orientation to the American community and to American education as related to their profession, as well as those professional people interested in the Seminar program who wish to spend the summer in a pleasant mountain region and in a congenial environment.
There will be lectures on America, American history and literature, government, and education. In addition to the members of the Black Mountain College faculty who will assist Professor Miller there will be several experienced tutors, some of whom have been on the staff of previous American Seminars, giving personal tuition and friendly counseling.
The location of the Seminar is unique. The College’s seven hundred acres of lake and mountain country lie in the heart of the Craggy Mountains of Western North Carolina, a section long renowned for its climate and beauty. An elevation of 2400 feet assures pleasant days and cool, refreshing nights. Asheville (15 miles away) and Black Mountain (6 miles away) have good stores, medical facilities and movies. There are many nearby places of interest: Great Smoky Mountains National Park, Mount Mitchell, Noris Dem and the TVA, and Blowing Rock.
As the College will be in session, members of the Seminar will be members of the College community and will be free to participate in all campus activities and social events, where they may enjoy informal contacts with students from many different parts of the country. At the College there is a lively interest in Art, Architecture, Drama, and Music. Members of the community regularly participate in instrumental and choral music and in dramatic productions. Hence players of musical instruments who are interested in informal ensemble playing should bring instruments with them. Access may be had to the small but well-selected College library and to records from the music library.
Community living: Members of the Seminar may also enjoy the opportunity to observe or to participate in the College farm, fell trees, clear pasture, and maintain and improve the building. Farm production for the war effort is being emphasized this summer and all members of the community are invited to contribute to this work.
Recreation: Seminar members may swim, hike, or play games (tennis, ping-pong, etc.) There are miles of roads and trails on or adjacent to the campus. There will be frequent dances and concerts.
Accommodations: The dormitories and dining hall are in buildings formerly used as a summer resort inn. Both men and women, single as well as married couples, will be accepted (no children). There is no age limit. Members will have room mates and will be expected to take care of their rooms. There will be a limited number of individual rooms for married couples. Each dormitory has a common room with large fireplace. Simple furnishings including bedding and towels are provided. Separate study facilities will be arranged for those who require opportunity for quiet concentration. The dining room, with a screened porch overlooking Lake Eden, will be managed by the College chef, and the kitchen work will be done by hired staff. The entire community, including faculty, students, and members of the Seminar, will eat together, at small tables, and will serve themselves.
Size: The Seminar will be limited to about fifteen or twenty members.
Fees: $110.00 per person for the six week session, covering all expense of room, board, and college tuition.
Railway connections: Black Mountain is situation directly on one of the main scenic routes of the Southern Railroad. Round trip fare from New York: Coach--$29.92; Pullman--$44.00 plus berth. Members will be met at the train by special car, at a charge of 50c. the best train leaves the Pennsylvania Station New York City at 4:35 P.M. with streamlined coaches and reserved seats which are free but must be reserved some time in advance. There is one change at Salisbury, N.C. The train arrives at Black Mountain at 8:35 A.M. Another train, leaving New York City at 2:05 P.M., arrives at Black Mountain also at 8:35 A.M. the next day.
Application: Because of limited enrollment, letters of application for admission to the Seminar on America should be sent promptly. Attach two copies of a brief statement listing personal data, nationality, professional training, experience, and special interests. Address: Professor Herbert A. Miller, Seminar on America, Black Mountain College, Black Mountain, North Carolina.
