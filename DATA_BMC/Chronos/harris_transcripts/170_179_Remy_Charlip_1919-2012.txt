MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION]. [LONG BLANK PAUSE] Okay. Remy, how did you
come to be at Black Mountain College?
RC: Well, I came because of Lou Harrison. I was living with him. Vera came and
asked him if he would like to come and teach at Black Mountain College. He
asked me if I’d like to come with him, and I said “Well”— I was, you know, at that
time starting to study with Merce and I didn’t want to leave the city. I think it
was—the first time I went down was at Thanksgiving. It was with M.C. Richards
and David Tudor, and we were in their car, which had a broken back window, a
door. We drove down. It was cold, but we were there for Thanksgiving and that
was my first experience there. That’s how I got to be there.
MEH: Did you have any expectations of what the college would be like? What had you
heard about it?
RC: Well, I actually had heard from a friend of Harriet Sohmers, her friend Mary Ann,
who wanted to go to Black Mountain College. She was a student at Cooper
Union School of the Arts where I was studying painting, and she had told me
about it, that it was this wonderful place. But I didn’t know anything about it, you
know, really, except that she thought it was a wonderful place.
