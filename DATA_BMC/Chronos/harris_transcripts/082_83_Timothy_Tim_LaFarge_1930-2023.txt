MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION] Tim, how did you hear about Black Mountain? How
did you come to be there?

heard about it because I went to a little rather liberal boys' prep school in New
Hope, Pennsylvania called Solebury School. It only had about sixty-five
students the years I was there. It grew. The year after I left, it merged. It bought
out a girls' school down the road named Holmquist. But anyway, in
Winter-Spring of '48 – well, probably earlier than that, the Fall of '47 – Mark and
I were looking at colleges. I wanted to go to a smaller college, so I applied to
Williams and Bowdoin and the third college for which I found a catalogue. They
had a closet full of college catalogues. They had a Black Mountain College
catalogue in the closet. I'd never heard of Black Mountain College. Didn't know
much about it at all. It was my third choice. But I was not a terribly good student
in those days, and my grades were not up to snuff, perhaps, or they were not
good enough anyway. My father drove me up to interview with the Dean of
Admissions at Williams College in the northwestern corner of Massachusetts –
