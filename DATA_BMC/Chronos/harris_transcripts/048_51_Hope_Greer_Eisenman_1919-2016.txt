MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION] Hope, how did you hear about Black Mountain?
HGE: The most important thing that sort of convinced me was, I believe Louis
Adamic, in a 1936 Harper’s magazine. I guess I must have heard about it. This
was a possibility for a college. This article really sold me.
MEH: Where were you living?
HGE: [OFFMIKE] I was living in Chappaqua, Westchester County. The other person
who told me about it was my husband Alvin. My present husband. He was
encouraging me to go to college. I had decided I didn't want to go to college
when I graduated from high school. I wanted to be a writer, but I didn't think that
you needed to go to college to be a writer. So, I started gathering experiences.
The experiences were ways to make money, because I really needed money.
Frist I was a salesgirl at Macy's, and then I got a job that was fun at Columbia
Concerts Corporation, being a receptionist, little jobs here and there. I was
gathering materials to be a writer. Then I was in the hospital for an appendix
operation when I suddenly sat back and thought – I realized I was missing
something, that most of my friends were going to college. Alvin, whom I had
known even at that time in high school, was at now Dartmouth. My friends were

HGE: No. In New York. I don't remember where it was, but, as I recall, it was John

thing where I just sat there and wrote. It would be more structured than I had
anticipated. This, of course, turned out to be absolutely wonderful. I needed it.
He was very gentle about it. He said, "You don't have to do it, but I want you to
try out this and that." The one thing that really was a bonus was he put me in
touch with Paul Radin, who was there then – the anthropologist. Paul Radin, I
had a tutorial with him as well as with Mangold, and he very soon involved me
in working on a book with him. For a young person that's pretty exciting. I knew
his reputation. I knew who he was. He convinced me that I was going to have a
book published, and he liked my writing. Then Fred and Paul got together on it,
and the book, of course, didn't get published.

I don't really know why, but we had different opinions about literature. The
classes were very nice and informal. We sort of sat around on the floor.
Fortunately, the person I really hit it off with was Fred Mangold, who was my
advisor. I also had a tutorial with him, a writing tutorial. He liked my writing very
much and encouraged me a lot and taught me a lot. Usually these things were
individual sessions, but Will Hamlin asked at one point if he could sit in on
them, because he wanted to hear what Fred Mangold had to say. I agreed that
that would be okay. So, then Will Hamlin did sit in on our tutorials.
