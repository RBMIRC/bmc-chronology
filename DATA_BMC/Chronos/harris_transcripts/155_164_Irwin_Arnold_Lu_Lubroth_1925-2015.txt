IL: Well, tell me the purpose of your book, because you've written one already. What
do you want to add?
MEH: Well, I'm interested in doing two things. I'm interested not only in recording
recollections for my own purpose now I've done my book, but for interests other
people might have in the future. That's one reason for doing this. The other
reason is I've interviewed many people and there was much material which I
could not use in the book. And so I'll probably be doing other books, publications,
in the future, about various—like a specific summer, or a specific theme or topic.
You never know what you'll learn, if you interview. It's always an open situation.
IL: I see. Yes. I'm interviewing you! Why have you devoted your life to this, as Ken
Snelson said?
MEH: I don't know. (LAUGHS)
IL: You don't know. I'm devoting my life right now to van Gogh. I'll tell you what I'm
trying to do.
MEH: You told me you were doing a book.

IL: I'm trying to simulate his landscapes through the camera lens, and it brings me to
France, which is not hard to take—Provence, near Paris where he died, and
Holland which is a lovely country, Nuenen and Zundert (his birthplace), places
like that. And other towns where he's been. Why I got engrossed in his life, I
really don't know. Certainly his painting intrigued me and his life intrigues
everybody, so I got involved and I really—I'm going to devote one weekend, a
month or ten days every four or five weeks, to following his footsteps. I didn't
bring my pictures, dammit!
MEH: Too bad. Are these the landscapes?
IL: Well they're just a few beginnings, and they're not landscapes—they're not
photos which will say, "My god, this guy's got a special eye," you know. They're
just very beautiful, I think very beautiful photographs.
MEH: These are color or black and white.
IL: Color, yeah, color. They're just poetry, let's say. It certainly isn't way out stuff, hm?
Not at all.
MEH: So, how did an architect become interested in doing this?
IL Because I feel that why the hell must I devote all my life to one profession? And
I've always been interested in photography—not deeply. Started at ID in Chicago.
And just picking it up again. I did a lot of it in Paris, and did—I would say
successfully, as an amateur. In other words, I won some contests—abstracts. But
now I'm devoting to realism. I'm trying to make them as beautiful as possible, and
there's enough things of van Gogh around where television antennas and
telephone wires do not interfere. There's a lot. I hope I can produce forty or fifty.
