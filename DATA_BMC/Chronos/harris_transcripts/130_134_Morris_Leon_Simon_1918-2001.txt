MEH: Mouse, how did you come to be at Black Mountain?
MS: The person who persuaded me was Louis Adamic from his book My America.
I’m not quite sure whether I first read about Black Mountain as his articles were
being written up monthly in Harper’s magazine monthly in 1936. I know
definitely that I read the book and was persuaded that it might be an interesting
place. I had gone to City College in New York right after graduation from high
school in [New] Jersey and lasted one semester. There were some difficulties.
My mother was dying and my father was in the store. I had to come back from
school in New York – going in the morning, come back in the evening. I was a
kid of seventeen years old. I tried out junior varsity, and I was down to one of
the last fifteen. I took a heavy course load. The result was that I was out of the
place in one semester. And then went to work in various places. But most
importantly in terms of Black Mountain and my own feeling, was a job I had with
Revlon Nail Enamel Corporation in New York City on 45th Street. They were
located between Sixth and Seventh Avenue. It was a factory job, and I was on
an assembly line. Got it in October 1937. Upon the death of my father in 1939,
he left four children three hundred dollars apiece from an insurance policy. I
don’t know how my brothers and my sister used their money. I know it was
perfect for me to apply to Black Mountain. That’s how I came to go to Black
Mountain. I wanted something a little different than what I had experienced in
City College although City College was a good place. If I were older and had
made a proper adjustment, it would have been a fine four years. At any rate I
came [read?] through Louis Adamic’s book in detail and was intrigued by it.
