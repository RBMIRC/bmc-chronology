MEH: What do you remember – I know that you really liked Bolotowsky's teaching.

WSA: He was a very gentle person. I felt a little bit under strain at first because I was
the only girl. They were all men or boys, in the class. I think of them as boys
now, but I guess they were men. But they were young. We were all young, but
they were all masculine. I got the feeling that they had been used to not having

criticism, but it was always objective. I never had any feeling there was
anything personal about it. It was always constructive, indicating what
directions he'd like for me to try to go in. It wasn't that he said, "That's bad!" I
mean "bad" doesn't tell you anything. He would make suggestions for things for
me to try. But he was always very gentle. He was very good about explaining
things to me. I asked him a lot of questions. I wanted to learn. In fact, I felt kind
of frantic about it since now I had just one semester. I was trying to cram as
much learning into that semester as I could to make up for lost time. I'd ask him
a lot of questions, and I tried a lot of things, and he went right along with me.
