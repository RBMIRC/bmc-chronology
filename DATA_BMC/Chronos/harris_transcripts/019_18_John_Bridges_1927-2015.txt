a horse of a different color, because they had very outstanding people,

any of the classes. I would go to concerts on Saturday night, and quite a
few Asheville people, really dyed-in-the-wool music lovers, would go to
those things. And those are the events that I remember going to see.
Outstanding in my memory is the Kolisch Quartet, you know, who gave
the first performances of many of the Schoenberg — in fact, he is

MEH: You've mentioned several musicians you particularly remember. Do you
remember any drama performances in particular?
JB: I remember a one-act version of the Molière The Ridiculous Young Ladies,
which they did, which was most innovative. The costumes were all made
out of crepe paper, variously colored. The lines of the costume were
authentic of the period, but the coloring and the decorations were very
fanciful and the tiered wigs of the men were made out of Shredded Wheat
biscuits, which had been glued to strips of cloth and then the whole thing
sprayed or painted, at that time — you'd have to make your own spray can
— pastel hues: pink, blue, green, that sort of thing. And, of course, the
make-ups were very fantastic. I remember that, and I remember The
Braggart Warrior, the Plautus play. I remember seeing that. And they
would do that in the dining hall, too. Their facilities for dramatic
presentation were poor in the extreme, so you concentrated on the acting
in it. I remember scenes from Ethan Frome. I never did see the whole
production of Ethan Frome, but I remember vividly the performances of
some of the people. There was a Jack Gifford, who was quite
extraordinary, and I remember one young woman named Renata Klepper.
