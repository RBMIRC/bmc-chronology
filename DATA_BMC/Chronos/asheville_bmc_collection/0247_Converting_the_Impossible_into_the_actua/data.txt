CONVERTING THE IMPOSSIBLE into the actual HAS ALWAYS BEEN AN AMERICAN TRADITION
NEEDED AT ONCE: $15,000
At stake: the growth of an idea
To an incredible extent imagination and determination can take the place of money. But they cannot produce cement and steel and glass and plywood.
The $15,000 needed to prepare adequate quarters for next year- to complete the main wing of the new building and remodel the old buildings- is a trifle compared with the sums colleges constantly request. And just for that reason it is harder to get.
People believe that so little is easy to get, that someone else will give it, that a few dollars will not help.
People fear that their contributions will be lost; and they wonder how money can be spent these days on anything except defense.
The College believes that $15,000 will be raised only is many people give small sums and if some people give larger ones. Every dollar will help substantially; everyone interested in the College can afford at least that amount.
If the College has existed eight years as a tenant, it can presumably exist many more years than that when established on its own property. Operating expenses will be decreased through the immediate saving of rent and through farming. There will no longer by the impermanence of renting which has kept away students, recognition, and support. A vicious circle will have been broken.
If a short-range view is taken, everything must go to defense. But in defending American should we not also defend American values and be prepared for the world of the future?
AN EXPRESSION OF THANKS to all those friends of Black Mountain College whose generous support in the past has enabled an idea to grow and to become a force in American education.
People said…
In 1933, that it was impossible to found a new college, to undertake a pioneer educational adventure, at the bottom of the depression
-without funds in sight
-without more than thirty teachers and students
-without a campus
For years, that it was impossible to operate a college controlled by faculty and students
-where close community life is practicable and beneficial
-where personal merit determines standing
-where freedom is a means to responsibility
In 1940, that it was impossible to build a modern, college plant using chiefly student and faculty labor
-with no money in sight
-with no technical experience
-with the pressure of having to move within a year
People say…
In 1941, that American education must adapt itself to a world in crisis
-by making itself as nearly as possible self-supporting
-by successfully combining practical and theoretical education
-by preparing resourceful citizens with democratic conviction in their bones
The growth of an idea… has been continuous at Black Mountain College for eight years. A simple, college plant is necessary to harbor that idea.
Where there was only a hillside in September, 1940, there is now a 75-room building, three-quarters completed. Some 16,000 man-hours of student and faculty labor, and some $19.000 in gifts, have brought it to this point. But it must be finished and the old buildings remodelled.
An appeal at a critical moment
Black Mountain College has often confronted the impossible, and, with the indispensable aid of its friends, has always converted the impossible into the actual.
Right now an impossibility must be overcome. The College is moving to its permanent home. Much work remains to be done to prepare adequate housing facilities for next year. This work can be done over the summer, with the aid of the Work Camp and with some hired labor.
But money is imperatively needed for materials. Educationally the College is stronger and more significant than ever before. The faculty and students intend to go on, whatever the circumstances. They fear, however, that too primitive and crowded conditions will jeopardize- and possibly obliterate- the values that eight years of unremitting effort have created.
They have done everything they can. And they believe in the adage about those who help themselves.
They appeal to you to help convert a seeming impossibility into an actuality.
