MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION]. How did you hear about Black Mountain? Where
were you?
FFB: I heard about it through my father. I had gone to Guilford College, which is a
Quaker school outside of Greensboro for a year, and knew I was not going to
go back there. It was not for me. During that summer, the summer of '45, my
dad said, "You really want to go to Black Mountain, don't you?" Not knowing too
much about it, really seldom hearing about it, I said, "Absolutely!" It was just
someplace different.
MEH: How had he heard about it?
FFB: He'd heard about it through Alex Reed, who was a neighbor.
MEH: This was in New Jersey?
FFB: Right. Alex lived across the river from us in Rumson, New Jersey, and he would
come in talk to my dad, who was the town pharmacist. Tell him of this place,
and my dad was fascinated and probably would have liked to have come
himself. So, he went through me. That was very satisfying to him. That's how I
got there.
MEH; Right.
FFB: In awe.

MEH: When you went, did you have any idea what you wanted to – Had you been to

FFB: No. No, never. I had spent probably – I can't remember whether it was summer
school at Juilliard, because my aunt taught piano in the preparatory division.

Saturday morning, for a little bit. That was the only contact I had with people of
that ilk, and it was very brief, very fleeting. But otherwise, no, I went to a local
high school – Rumson High School – which was very good in music. It did
produce two very fine popular musicians, Nelson Riddle and Bill Finegan, who
also had his own popular orchestra. So, music was very important there, and I
sang there. But otherwise, it was a very normal, regular, small town high
school.

FFB: I was going to do creative writing, which is what I did at Guilford, and I was very

were working on the Brahms Requiem, which was fortuitous, maybe, because

FFB: Well, I lived in the lodge. I lived – Originally, I lived downstairs, and I lived with
three other females. Then I moved upstairs – I guess that must have been the
second year and lived in the attic with a whole bunch of girls. That was fun. So,

scene?" I got very, very interesting answers from the different people. I
remember Max Dehn saying, "Well, what you do is you draw a picture, and you
show exactly how high the trees are and how big the lake is, and so forth and
so on." I remember the final answer I got, "Oh, you can't, you can't describe it,

answers, very typical of their disciplines. It was kind of fun. But you'd walk up,
and you'd get busy, and you'd either go to class or get ready to go to class. Go
to your study. I guess mornings were mostly in classes. I took some silly
classes and some wonderful classes. I took shorthand.
