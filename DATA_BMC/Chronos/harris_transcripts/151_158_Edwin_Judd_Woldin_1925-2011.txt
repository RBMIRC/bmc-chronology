MEH: You went to Black Mountain in the fall of '44?
JW: Right.
MEH: How did you come to be there?
JW: I remember having difficulty getting into a college. I had a very irregular
academic background, and I think I'd kinda’ been thrown out of a high school

educa tional system. Of course, that was a very anti-intellectual environment
where you learn not to know all the right answers or carry a violin case, And
the problem, which I still have, of, you know, kind of lack of structure or
self-discipline was very acute then. But obviously I wanted to go on. I think I
had been playing with jazz—I had left high school to play with jazz bands and
was very ambitious to study music seriously and also to study architecture. I'm
not—I think at that time—I tend to say that I wanted to be an architect then, but
I'm not sure that I wasn't in just a conflicting situation about the two things.
Black Mountain just seemed like a most exciting place for somebody who on
