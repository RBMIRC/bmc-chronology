MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION] Marianne, you were at Black Mountain in the
summer of 1953?
MS: Right.
MEH: How did you come to be there?
MS: Well, I had been dan- – I had met Merce Cunningham in probably the spring of
1949 in Paris. He came and he did a studio performance with I think it was
Tanaquil LeClerq and Betty Nichols. I had a friend who was staying at the same
hotel that Merce was in. I went to the studio performance and was very
impressed. I had seen him dance before in New York, and I heard that he
wanted a studio to work in so I sat outside of his hotel until he (LAUGHS)
appeared and then told him that I knew a place that he could use, and it was a
studio that we were taking classes in. I was studying at a drama school that
Jean-Louis Barrault had founded, called Education par Le Jeu Dramatique. We
had a young woman Jacqueline Levane, who was teaching dance, and she
was very new at it, and so she was delighted to have him teach the classes.
So, that was how I met him. He taught classes through the late spring and in

MS: Well, I was going to Cornell University, which is where I was studying dance

was all, you know, out. A lot of fast, fast work. Always difficult. It was always
challenging, and trying to – You know, he would do, he would do things that he
was working on for pieces, little parts of them in the class, and we had to learn
them. That was always very challenging to learn these very intricate pieces of
work.

MEH: Did he have a studio then? Where were you practicing?
MS: Well, he would rent different studios. I don't know. They were all in the Village. I
can't remember where the first one was. I think it was somewhere on Eighth
Street. They were studios that he would rent from different dancers. He didn't
get his own studio – I mean his own studio, until he moved into Westbeth,
which was after I was gone already.
MEH: Quite late.
MS: Yeah, but there were, there were a number that he had for longer periods of
time. The last one that I remember was on Christopher Street– Sheridan
Square. Nobody else used that, as far as I know. I think he had that himself for
a number of years. We did a lot of work there.
MEH: Was he working with Tudor then as accompanist? Or did that come later?
MS: Yeah. No, I don't think I remember meeting David Tudor until Black Mountain.
MEH: What about Cage?
MS: Cage was there. He would play sometimes for classes. He was always – He
was very – He was a very warm and lovely person to be around. He was
always very attentive. Merce was much more temperamental, especially in
those years. He would sometimes be in a funk of some kind, and sometimes
he'd be very outgoing. I think it was very hard, because they were scraping by
on nothing. I mean, once in a while I'd get my father to take Merce out to dinner
with us. He'd have a good meal. I mean it was like – It was really hard for them,
so I'm not surprised that he was struggling.
MEH: I think it was hard financially but artistically, too.
