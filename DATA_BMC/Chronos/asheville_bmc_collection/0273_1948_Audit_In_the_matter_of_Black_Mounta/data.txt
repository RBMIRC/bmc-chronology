William Morse Cole
Professor of Accounting, Emeritus
Harvard University
Cambridge, Massachusetts
November 20, 1948
In the matter of Black Mountain College
To whom it may concern:
I have today completed the fifteenth annual detailed audit of the accounts and records of the Corporation of Black Mountain College, I have examined its books of account, its records, and its property, have lived in one of its residential halls, eaten in its commons, and participated as far as time would allow in its activities during the conduct of the audit. For the fifteenth time I find its books and records carefully kept. Since during the fiscal year 1947-48 a certified public accountant was in charge of its financial office, I have not deemed a detailed audit, such as that heretofore made by me, to be necessary. I have examined with care the accounting methods used, and feel justified in using the resulting figures for drawing up the following statements.
I hereby certify that the accompanying statements (I, Summary Statement of Income and Expenses for the tenth year of its existence, September 1, 1947- August 31, 1948), and II, Summary Statement of Financial Condition, August 31, 1948), are in accord with its books of account as of the dates named, and, in my judgement, give a correct picture of its finances.
(Signed) William Morse Cole

An appreciable part of the property, impossible to evaluate exactly, is not represented here. The amount here shown is the cost to the College, but does not include any figure for the labor of students or faculty, though both students and faculty worked part time for many months, over several years, under the supervision of an experienced architect member of faculty and of an experienced paid builder. This has been part of the regular college athletic activities, an element in cultivation of community spirit, and a factor in establishing an appreciation of some of the problems of labor. The economic value of such unpaid labor is suggested by the fact that professional appraisers set a figure, for insurance purposes, of over $150,000 for the buildings owned by the College at that time. Additions have been made since.
This property consists of 667 acres, formerly used for a summer hotel, with several buildings, including a common hall, dormitories and cottages. The property was appraised, soon after purchase, by the Asheville Real Estate Board, at $70,567. A part of the original purchase has been transferred to the Farm, which is operated as an independent unit of the College.
Since the Corporation has no capital stock, these items indicate the amount of its proprietorship.
