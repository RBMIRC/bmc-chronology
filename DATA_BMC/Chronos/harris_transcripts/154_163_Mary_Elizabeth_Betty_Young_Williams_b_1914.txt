investigation [OVERTALK], and all of that through Black Mountain College.

BYW: Well, heavens!

BYW: I sort of remember everything about it. I mean they—

him, because he was somewhat controversial. I can't—terrible. So, then when he
appealed to the AAUP, then they had to marshal all these charges against him.

objected to the noise that the class next door was making, and he said that the
teacher reminded him of something that rhymed with "witch." He said at some

MEH: Before we go to Black Mountain, at Rollins do you have any other memories of
the whole fracas concerning his dismissal?
BYW: Well, it occupied us solely for what seemed to be like months, I don't know. Of
course, there were all the other faculty members who left sort of in sympathy. I'm
not entirely sure—at this point I can't remember whether some of them were fired
or whether they all just left out of disaffection.
MEH: I think some of both. But, I think the only ones of those—Like, I didn’t know Mr.
Lounsbury at all. I sort of did know Dr. Georgia. But, Ted Dreier I knew somewhat
as a young member of the faculty. His wife and children.
MEH: Did you know other students who decided to join the Black Mountain group?
BYW: Well, yes, Nan Chapin. Who else came from Rollins? Nat French, who I was
dating my second year at Rollins. He was the chairman of the student body then,
president of the student body. Or, he won an election. He and George Barber.
George never came to Black Mountain, but he was kind of associated with it.
It seems to me that maybe Nan was the only one—Sally Sylvester, whom I had
not heard of in a million years—she got in touch with me fairly recently, not too
long before she died. [SPEAKING TO DOG--NOT TRANSCRIBED]. I often wish I
had a list of who all the people were.
MEH: What was your first impression on arriving at Black Mountain?
BYW: My parents drove me down, and I guess my first impression was of that great
enormous reception room there. I don't know. I loved it from the beginning, from
the very beginning. I never had a moment’s disaffection or anything of that sort.

visiting in the girls' rooms, and so we had one of the famous community meetings

that what was arousing people like the Georgias and so on was the fact that
there were sheets on the bed. Sheets on the beds made it a bedroom and
therefore made an unwarranted amount of intimacy.
