MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION]. How did you come to be at Black Mountain
College? How did you hear about it?
ASL: Well, I was in New York, and I had graduated from Hunter College as an art
student. I had always read about Black Mountain. I was at that point particularly
interested in going into textile design. Of course, Anni Albers, you know, I'd
always heard about her being down there, et cetera. Except by the time I got
there, they had already left. But they did have somebody else who had taken
her place and was using her methods for teaching. But I liked – I just bought
the whole concept of Black Mountain. I guess Hunter College was a very sort of
conservative art school where you always learned art in a certain way. I liked
the freedom that Black Mountain would have given you to explore things on
your own with teachers who were quite famous who would also be like mentors
for you. So, that appealed to me a lot.
MEH: At Hunter, you had heard about Black Mountain.
ASL: Oh, yes.
MEH: How was it known at the – What was its reputation?
ASL: Oh, it was known as a very avant garde school.
