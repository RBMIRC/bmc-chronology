MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION] Carol, how did you hear about Black Mountain
College?
CSK: When I was in high school, I knew that I wanted to do something in the art field
and I applied to Cooper Union and Bard College and places like that. My art
teacher said, "There's a wonderful place I would like you to apply to. It's in
North Carolina. It's called Black Mountain College" —which I had obviously
never heard of. So, I applied and I sent in —you know, they sent me all the
information and I sent in a pastel drawing, and lo and behold they accepted me.
MEH: And you were still in high school?
CSK: Yes. I was seventeen.
MEH: And you were —Did you say Cooper —
CSK: I applied to Cooper Union.
MEH: You applied to Cooper Union.
CSK: Right. I took the first half of the exam and I got sick for the second half, and at
that point I was interested in Black Mountain. I had been hooked so I never
pursued the rest of it.
MEH: And where were you in high school?

MEH: Okay. So you went. How did your parents feel about this?
CSK: Well, you have to understand. I'm the youngest in a family of four children. The
youngest by ten years —the baby of the family. Everybody's baby. They were a
little hesitant but I was very surprised they let me go! There was some
hesitation on their part, but they did let me go, I think with the encouragement
of my older brothers and my sister. I don't know whether they were sorry. I think
they were sorry in the end, but (LAUGHS) that's another story.
MEH: Okay. But you went. How did you get there?
CSK: Took the train from I guess it was from Penn. I'm not sure. I don't know whether
it was Penn or Grand Central. On the train I met Kendall Durant and Julie Light
and Sasha —Shirley Dickenson. Somebody else, I'm not sure —Felix, I think,
Felix —
MEH: Krowinski?
CSK: Yes. I think he was on the train. I'm not sure. That was my first meeting with any
of these people, obviously, and it was so fortunate that I met them going down
there. We got there. It was overnight —and I must say that I was very shocked
when I got out of the train coming from New York because the first thing I saw
was Colored Fountain, White Fountain, Colored Restroom, White Restroom. It's
one thing to hear it, and another thing to see it. It was very strange. At any rate,
we were picked up and taken to the school and showed our rooms and
everything. I was standing in the middle of my room. I was fortunate, I had a
corner room.

CSK: Yeah, in the Lodge, right, North Lodge. As you face the Lodge, it was the

MEH: Did you feel comfortable?
CSK: Well I think —You have to understand, this was my first time away from home.
Being the baby of the family and very sheltered and very protected, I was very
shy. I didn't (LAUGHS) eat in the dining room for two days because I was too
shy to go in and sit down among people I didn't know. Then I finally kind of
broke the ice and sat down I don't remember with whom. But it took a couple of
days for me to become adjusted to it.
MEH: Did you know what you wanted to study?
CSK: Yes, I knew that I wanted to study art. I had been disappointed when I found out
that Albers wasn't going to be there for the fall term, but Ilya Bolotowsky was
wonderful. He was a wonderful substitute, and he was such a warm and caring
man. You got the feeling that he was really interested in the work that you did.
So, I was very —I was pleased. As disappointed as I was that Albers wasn't
there at the time, I was pleased with Ilya.

MEH: So how did you go about selecting your courses?
CSK: I'm a little hazy on that, to be honest with you. Obviously, I knew that I wanted
the art courses. I wanted the lit courses with M.C., who is wonderful. She
opened —That's the one thing about Black Mountain. It opened your head, it
opened your mind to all kinds of things, and M.C. was very instrumental in the
literature end of it and introducing us to all kinds of things. Things at least that I
had never experienced before —poets, writers. Of course, she did
Shakespeare wonderfully also. Then I also took a course —and I don't
remember which course it was —with Max Dehn. It must have been philosophy.
He was wonderful. I just kind of fell into everything.
MEH: So where were classes held? Did you have like a regular schedule of classes?
CSK: Yes, as I recall, we did. We did. For some reason I'm hazy about the schedule
itself. It was obviously every day. Then we had the Work, the Work Program
also, which was a hoot. (LAUGHS)

CSK: Shoveled coal. [LAUGHTER] I remember we went down —This was aside
from, you know, the kitchen detail and everything. I remember we had The Coal
Detail. We went down to the freight yards, and I don't remember who it was
—was it Harry Holl? Somebody, one of the guys, and I see this great big freight
car and I have to climb to the top of that thing. I think it was Harry said to me,
"Anybody that's that afraid could never fall." (LAUGHTER) So, I did it. We went
up there and shoveled the coal, and then got back to school and had to shovel
it into the —

CSK: Yes he returned in —Actually it was, I believe it was January when we got back

MEH: You said that your not being able to go back really changed your life. How do
you think that really affected you, besides just —
CSK: I would hope that I would have blossomed there, that perhaps I would have, I

ways. But I still am very grateful for the time I did have there, and sorry that it
doesn't exist anymore. There were a lot of —We had a lot of good times there
too.

MEH: Did you come prepared for the informal dress style at Black Mountain?
CSK: Yes, to a certain degree. Let me tell you how I came by that. First of all, Merv
told me a little bit about it. Second of all, my father was a schoolteacher, and we
didn't have that much money. My parents asked if they could, if Black Mountain
could reduce the price a little bit. I don't remember exactly what it was that they
were asking to begin with. My mother sent a list of things like I needed a certain
amount for clothing, et cetera. We got a letter back from I think it was Mr.
Rondthaler, and he said, "We do not live in a very dressy style here in Black
Mountain. We live very casually, so not many clothes are required." So, I was
more or less prepared. I had jeans and stuff like that. I didn't mind. That was
fine with me. I didn't mind not getting dressed up. I loved it.
MEH: How was the food?
CSK: The food wasn't bad. I remember —Cornelia? Was it Cornelia? Malrey? They
were delightful. They were wonderful. The food wasn't bad. I wasn't used to it
though. It was Southern cooking, and it was a lot of creamy stuff. I tried grits.
That was it! (LAUGHS) Once was enough. Stuff that I wasn't really used to. As
a matter of fact, I put on a tremendous amount of weight there. But the food
wasn't bad. I mean I don't remember it badly. I remember some dishes that I
had never tasted before that I liked, but I just wasn't used to it at first. But
