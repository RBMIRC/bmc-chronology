Cambridge, Massachusetts
December 10, 1941
In the matter of Black Mountain College
To whom it may concern:
I have today completed my eighth  annual audit of the accounts and records of the Corporation of Black Mountain College, covering the transactions of its eighth year of existence, September 1, 1940, to August 31, 1940, and I find them kept not only with technical bookkeeping correctness and accuracy for, gifts have been scrupulously applied to the purposes for which they were given, and, on the strength of my seventh annual extended residence in the College, I for the seventh time report its expenditures justified by its educational policy.
The necessity of removing from its original location, in leased quarters, was foreseen four years ago, and the present Lake Eden property was then purchased. The buildings were not fully adequate for college purposes, and extensive improvements and new construction were imperative. An experienced professor of architecture, trained in the technique of construction as well as in design, was soon added to the faculty. One of his functions has been the planning and direction and supervision of improvements and new construction carried on in part by local workmen, under an experienced builder, and in part by the student body, which uses this opportunity for practical work on the property as physical exercise, as training in handicraft, and as means of enlightenment on the problems of labor. In the auditing point of view, all this, which I have had good opportunity to observe, means that the work at Lake Eden has been accomplished at an expense many thousand dollars less than under usually prevailing conditions, because no architect’s fee, no contractor’s profit, and no wage expense for student labor (which has proved notably good), have been involved.
I hereby certify that the accompanying statements (I. Summary Statement of Income and Expenses, and II. Summary Statement of Financial Condition) are in accordance with its books of account as of the dates indicated, and give, in my judgement, a correct picture of its finances.
(Signed) William Morse Cole
