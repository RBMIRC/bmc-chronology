MEH: Okay, when you were at Black Mountain you were Ursula Lewis?
UM: Yes, that's right. That was my maiden name, and that is – we adopted the name
because you can hear by my accent that I'm not an American. It was actually –
I was in Black, at Black Mountain College through reading an article in the New
York Times. My mother was then very supportive, read this and said, "You must
apply for a scholarship.” I show you the article. I have it in my book here.
_M: At the time you were studying where?
UM: I was studying at the Mannes, and, in fact I remember, this comes back to me,
the old Mrs. Mannes, David and Clara Mannes, the founders of the Mannes,
what is now the Mannes College, were bringing me over to this country. I was
here just a few years, and I was finished. We didn't get degrees in those years.
I was finished with my education at Mannes, studied with George Szell. They
were very upset that I went to Black Mountain College because this was an
extension of the education, they thought, for selfish reasons [LAUGHS], didn't
think that I shouldn't go anywhere anymore.

Schoenberg's later twelve-tone music, but it was difficult enough, I suppose, to
get people to listen to that music. Edward Steuermann, whose daughter now –
it's so funny how this comes up in life – is playing cello very well, is playing in
pieces of mine at the group called Alaria, Alaria Chamber Ensemble. Her father
