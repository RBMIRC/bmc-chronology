MEH: You were at Black Mountain in the summer of 1944?
MD: That is correct.
MEH: How did you come to be there?
MD: That was one of my first questions I asked upon my arrival! It— I was
closely connected with a great number of the faculty that were there, and
when I arrived, Jalowetz, these are the whole bunch, handed out
schedules for the week activities for the whole summer: "You are going to
take this course, that class, and so on." There was none for me. So I
asked him what, why am I here, what I'm supposed to do here? His
answer was, I remember it just about verbatim, he said that the program,
the title of this gathering in the summer is interpretation, "and it is just
unimaginable to have a course on interpretation and not have you
participating. But don't worry, you will be plenty busy. You will see. Three
years from now you won't know any more what happened." [LAUGHS]
And so it was. Before you put on that tape, I mention to you that three,
four, five girls, almost every morning came at five o'clock to make

Lowinsky, for instance, was very radically for admitting Negro students, so

their opinion, and their suggestion was quite interesting. Did anyone else
