MEH: [GIVES IDENTIFICATION]. And you were at Black Mountain, according to
my records for two years, from 1945 to 1947.
JM: Yes.
MEH: How did you hear about Black Mountain?
JM: Let's see. I had been at Wheaton up in Norton, Mass., for two years —
and unhappily, because it seemed very structured and whatnot. We were
adventurous, and I knew I had to make a change. It was during the War,
and the only other place I was interested in was Bennington, but
Bennington was just exactly what I had. It was all girls. Unendurable
situation.
MEH: Was Wheaton all girls?
JM: All girls. And so was Bennington, and I wasn't going to put up with that
again. So, god knows how I heard of Black Mountain. I don't know to this
day. But that was that. And that's how I came to go.
MEH: Did you have any — Did you have a progressive background? Had you —
Were you a believer in progressive education?

JM: Not too much, but I think that there — I've been thinking about it ever

art school.
