MEH: Do you remember how you physically traveled to Black Mountain the first
time?
CH: I went on the bus. And I went from New Hampshire to Florida to Asheville
to Black Mountain, and I got there about in—Black Mountain town about
three in the morning, I think, and they let me off at the post office and the
grocery store there. And I didn’t know—I didn’t know how to get to the
college at all. I didn’t know where it was or anything and I—There was a
big stack of newspapers in front of the post office, and a man came along
to get them because he had to deliver them. So that was his job, so I said
“Do you think I could ride with you, because I have to go over to the
college? ”And he said, “Oh I don’t get over there until much
later—practically eight o’clock in the morning. ”And I said, “Well, that’s all
right. I don’t know what else to do. ” So we rode around all night, and he
taught me how to fold up newspapers and throw them. [LAUGHS] And we
went to Montreat and all about, and finally he took me over to Black
Mountain to the college. I got out at the dining hall. And I’d been up all
night and met some very nice people. And I think then I went to bed.
MEH: Where did you live? Where did your family live? You had been in Windsor
Mountain School in Connecticut.
CH: In Massachusetts. Let’s see. Well, my family sort of busted up at that
point. I came sort of from Swarthmore, Pennsylvania. And I think my
mother and my stepfather at that point were in Marion, Pennsylvania,
taking care of my grandmother. But they were busting up, too. So I didn’t
really live anywhere except at Windsor Mountain and then at Black
Mountain.
MEH: Do you remember your first impression of the college when you finally

kinds of folded paper sculpture that—some marvelous thing that—what’s
the Japanese girl?
MEH: Ruth Asawa?
CH: Ruth Asawa had done. Yes. It was in the dining hall, and it was wonderful.
And it seemed—Everything seemed, you know, well what would you say?
Obviously they had wonderful design there. Well, she’s just practically a
magician anyway, that woman. I loved the grounds, and I don’t remember
what else I saw. And, of course, the Studies Building was fascinating: to
have a study of your own! And I’d been primed on that one, about how
