MGH: Geissbuhler is German-Swiss, and my father was Swiss. I was born in

MGH: I came as a baby. We used to go back and forth every year until after the
Depression. Then there was no more money so we stayed here.

MGH: My parents were – We always had a place here on Cape Cod, and we spent
the summers here and lived in Cambridge. I grew up in Cambridge,
Massachusetts, and my father taught at Wellesley. Then when I graduated from
high school I moved to New York, because my father was a sculptor. He wasn't

MEH: So how did you come to be at Black Mountain College?
MGH: Well, I had gone to a very strict girls' private school.

MEH: Do you remember what year it was?
MGH: I think it was 1947. I went in January or February, wherever the break was. It
was one of the few times I saw snow there. There was snow all over. It was
very gray, and the lake was frozen. So, I came in the middle of the year and

MEH: So what was your first impression of the college?
MGH: Well, having had such a controlled life – everything I did was either controlled
by my school or my mother. My first thing was that it was beautiful. The second
thing was that I met M.C. She was to be my advisor. I walked into her study for
my first appointment with her and was going to outline my curriculum for the
courses I was going to take. She was sitting cross-legged on the floor, and she
said, "Well, whaddya wanta do?" I said, "Well I don't really know." I was just
taken aback. I guess I hadn't understood exactly what the – that there was
going to be so much freedom.
MEH: How were you dressed to go down? You started to say.
MGH: How was I dressed? Oh, my gosh, I had on high heels and silk stockings and I

worked for them. I mean Harry became a potter, and the other two guys – Harry
