STUDENT-COMMUNITY BULLETIN BLACK MOUNTAIN COLLEGE
June Bulletin 1945 college year 12
EVENTS:
June 14- Peggy Kurtz left the College this evening to join her husband, Kenneth, in New Haven. After visiting some of the members of their families, the Kurtzes will return to New Haven- Peggy to work and keep house, Kenneth to begin the writing of his doctorate thesis.
June 15- Lt and Mrs Don Kelley left for Washington, DC, after a two weeks’ visit. The Kelleys came not only to see us in action but to take part in our activities. They hope to start a school of their own after the war and are formulating an educational philosophy on the basis of what they have seen of several schools.
June 16- the Spring Quarter of the 1944-45 Session ended at 12:30 noon.
June 17- At a morning meeting of the students remaining at the College, it was decided that we should have an interim government until the beginning of the Summer Quarter on June 26. Meeting again in the afternoon, the students elected the following interim government: Student Officers: Vera Baker, Chuck Forberg, Helen Rosenbluth; Student Moderator: Irma Ehrman.
June 18- ‘Vacation’ began. About a dozen students remain to work with Paul Beidler, Mac Wood and Molly Gregory on the new service quarters, which, it is hoped, will be finished this weekend; on the practice cubicles, at least one of which is expected to be ready for use at the opening of the Institutes; on the farm and various general crews. A pool of all student labor will clean the Studies Building and the lodges when the June rains do not permit outdoor work.
FROM THE EDITORS:
Dr Miller, speaking at a community meeting on June 13, emphasized that “the hurricane which has run through the history of the year has swept every human being into its periphery...We are all suffering from the birth pains of a new era...The world has both expanded and contracted while we have been sitting in this mountain valley. Our neighbors are not only along the North Fork Road; they are in Russia, China, Iran...”
In our evaluations of this year, we will remember that since this session began, the world has seen the advancement of the Allied invasion of Europe, a presidential election of world importance, the conferences of Dumbarton Oaks, Yalta and San Francisco inaugurating the planning of a new world order, the death of President Roosevelt, the annihilation of fascist leaders and the smash-up of the Nazi machine.
The 1944-45 Session at Black Mountain College, as at every other college, took place in the shade of these events. Under the impact of history, we here studied, worked, laughed, discussed and were discussed. We have had crises, which seem indispensable to the continued vitality of the College; we have argued, despaired and achieved.
We will remember the work of our graduates- Jane Slater’s matieres, Widget Bauer’s textile exhibits, Betty Kelley’s children’s play. We will remember the arrival at tea of a telegram announcing Betty’s success in her examinations at Chapel HIll. We will remember graduation candidate Sam Brown conducting some of his on compositions for his trial examination.

We will remember Samuel Guy Inman wittily and wisely reporting on the Chapultepec Conference, Frank Graham explaining the work of the War Labor Board, Ira Reid talking on the problems of the South.
We will remember Mendez Marks visiting and poetizing from study to study, Fred Mangold in the money-raising campaign for the Institutes, Paul Beidler and his wife and four children trooping down the hill to breakfast.
We will remember the exciting exhibitions of art work; the student and faculty meetings at which we agreed to admit to the College regular session a Negro teacher or student and two Negro students to the Institutes; Alfred Kazin cabling that he will return to teach here in the fall; the modern dance program which climaxed the Winter Quarter interlude; the Valentino’s party, the theme of which was “stripes”; our winning of highest honors at the Festival of the Carolina Dramatic Association; the announcement of the summer Institutes guest faculties.
All of these activities and people made up the 1944-45 Session, and more. But trying to define the ‘more’ is trying to define Black Mountain College this year or any year. We here are too close to dare try. we hope that living in the shade has not stunted our growth and that we have continued to develop the Black Mountain hardiness, generosity, vitality and enthusiasm.
A NOTE TO READERS:
At a recent Faculty meeting, the proposal of two students and a member of the staff to publish the bulletin as a student activity was read and tentatively accepted. According to this proposal, Betty Osbourne will supervise the format of the bulletin, Irma Ehrman will edit the contents and Helsie Marden will mimeograph, collate and send it out.
If this first issue in the opinion of the Faculty and our readers, successful, we will continue doing it. We ask for your comments, for your contributions- such as poems, short essays or personal opinions on the College- and most especially for your continued correspondence with the bulletin. Address all mail to the Student Community Bulletin. Your opinions, discussions, ideas and suggestions are not only welcome, but needed.
June 20, 1945
