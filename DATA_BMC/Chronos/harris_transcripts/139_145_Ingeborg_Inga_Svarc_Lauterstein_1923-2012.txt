MEH: [IDENTIFICATION GIVEN] Inge, how did you come to be at Black Mountain?
ISL: By pure chance. I saw an article in Life magazine and liked the picture of
students sitting up in the rafters during a lecture. Then I read that several
famous European modern artists were on the faculty. Quite different from the
Academy of Fine Art in Vienna. A very conservative institution. Had they
accepted Adolf Hitler, he might have become a bad academic painter and the
world would have been spared. Had they accepted me, I would never have
written to Black Mountain College. So, after a correspondence and letters of
recommendation back and forth, I was accepted.
MEH: You saw this Life magazine in Vienna?
ISL: I was in the AAmerican reading room waiting for an American friend who
worked for the Red Cross. I showed her the article and she had a co-worker
who came from North Carolina. Her friend, an African-American young woman,
gave me the Black Mountain College address and told me how beautiful the
country was, but she never said anything about racial discrimination and
segregation. Black Mountain College was the only desegregated college at that

MEH: Going back. I want to go back further to the beginning again. You said that you
were in Vienna, you lived in Vienna through the war.
ISL: No, actually not. I was spirited away to safety. But I came back during the early
occupation of Vienna which was pretty hazardous because there wasn’t much
food and people had to just go scrounging around for black market food. Of
course, the Russians were occupying Vienna, and, as I describe it in my books,
there was a sense of imminent danger because they had surrounded Vienna.
They were in Czechoslovakia and Hungary and Yugoslavia. These were all
Communist countries, so that Vienna was isolated and connected only by the
Mozart Express Train and the airport which the Russians closed during at night.
So, that even the Allied forces were actually in a little mouse trap. Everything
was completely surrounded. All old imperial regions were now in the hands of
the Communists. So, in Vienna, there was this psychological turmoil where
everyone quickly turned old-Austrian. The old titles and the monarchists and
the old fine manners and the balls. There were balls before the streetcars were
running. People walked in evening clothes to the big balls in the center of
Vienna.
MEH: Why did they do this?
ISL: Because they wanted to be old Austrian again. They wanted their dignity back
because they had been shamed by the whole thing. So, it was a climate where
young people wanted to flee. I described it, of course, in Vienna Girl, the story
of one girl who took a backpack and simply walked towards Hamburg. And then

that you hadn’t done.

MEH: When you got to Black Mountain, what classes did you choose to take?

I loved him once I met him. But I really wanted everything American. The first
