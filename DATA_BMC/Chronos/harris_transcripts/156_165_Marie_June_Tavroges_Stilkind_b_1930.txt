MEH: Ruth Asawa, of course.
MTS: Ruth Asawa and Elaine Urbain.
MEH: Right. I’m in touch with all of these people.
MTS: Yeah.
MEH: Great but how did you meet him [Ray] in New York?
MTS: I met him through Norman Solomon, who introduced me to Philip Glass, who
introduced me to Albert M. Fine, who introduced me to Ray Johnson. So it
was by way of Norman. I needed an apartment. I was desperate for an
apartment. I had ten days to find one. And I saw Norman and he knew that
Phil Glass was leaving his to get a house downtown—a big loft. So then I was
introduced to Philip Glass. And when I met Phil, Albert was there. Albert was
there—Albert M. Fine. And Albert and I became friends. You know, I was
friendly with Phil Glass also but not as close as with Albert. And, of course, he
introduced me to Ray Johnson and then we had this New York
Correspondence School. And I was at Juilliard. I was working at Juilliard. And,
of course—
MEH: In the mail room or—-?
MTS: No, no. I was editorial assistant on the alumni magazine. But this is how, you
know, I kept seeing Albert and Phil. Philip Glass was—he was a friend but not
really on the circuit. He was not with the New York Correspondence School.
He was, you know, doing other things. But he was around. I mean, you know
who Phil Glass is?
MEH: Right. Definitely. Sure. Ok. I hope this tape is—

MTS: Bob Heide—Dorothy Podber. You know, there was a whole gang of them.
MEH: Right.
MTS: But Ray and Albert and I were sort of like the Three Musketeers. We’d go to
gallery openings and parties and that sort of thing. I used to hang around with
them. You know, like the three of us used to go out a lot.
MEH: Right.
MTS: And one evening—I guess it was about one in the morning—we were coming
home from somewhere, and we came across one of the compositions and
composers, you know. Composing—not composing, conducting. It was
James Levine. And he’s now, you know, with the Metropolitan Museum. I
don’t know whether Ray followed up on that or not. But Ray used to—he’d
meet somebody, and he’d sort of carry on with it.
MEH: Right. Alright, well, this thing is going to be so brief that I really don’t—
MTS: You don’t need all these stories.
MEH: Well, no, I would really like—I hope they’re recorded and I’d really like to
pursue several avenues further but right now, because it’s late, I’m going to
focus just now on getting this preface done. But I want to be sure that I
have—that I mention in the preface at least briefly the people who were at
Black Mountain that he, you know, was closely associated with, both those he
met there and in New York and to be clear, you know, on just how the
association came about.
MTS: The association was ex-Black Mountain College people in New York, living all
in the same area.
