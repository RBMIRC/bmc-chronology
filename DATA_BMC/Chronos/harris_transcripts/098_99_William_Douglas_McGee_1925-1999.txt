MEH: How did you come to be at Black Mountain?
WMG: Well, I was at the University of New Mexico. I was going to college out there,
and I was an undergraduate. And I was majoring in art. And I came across
some, you know—literature on the college in the library. And I began to read
all of the literature, and I found that I was very interested in the attitudes there
and philosophy and whatnot. And so I began to correspond with the college.
And I wrote to—That's when I first knew of Hazel Larsen. And she was at the
time the registrar. And so I wrote these letters expressing how much I wanted
to go there. And I was interested in their summer—the so-called summer
institutes. And so she would write back, and she'd write marvelous long
letters—two-page letters, three-page letters sometimes—explaining their
position and what they were trying to do there and all that sort of thing. And so
I just got gradually more and more excited about going there. And then, of
course, it was climaxed by the fact that I have become very interested in the
paintings of Franz Kline. And it was strictly second-hand, through art
magazines and whatnot, because I was not in New York. And then when I
learned he was going to be resident artist there in the summer of '52, that
pushed me over the top in terms of trying to get there in '52. And I had

WMG: Well, it's funny, Mary. There wasn't much specific conversation in terms of

believe that Black Mountain was a kind of—like a pot boiling in—With
everything else going on, you know. It was—painting was just one more

kind of very progressive position that the college represented. You know? And
there weren't too many people there that really understood much about
painting, that I could see. There was just a sympathy, a lot of sympathy about it.
