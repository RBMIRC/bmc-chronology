William Morse Cole
Professor of Accounting, Emeritus
Harvard University
Cambridge, Massachusetts
November 5, 1942
In the matter of Black Mountain College
To whom it may concern:
I have today completed the ninth annual audit of the accounts and records of the Corporation of Black Mountain College, covering the transactions of its seventh year of existence, September 1, 1941, to August 31, 1942, and I find them kept not only with technical bookkeeping correctness and accuracy for, gifts have been scrupulously applied to the purposes for which they were given, and, on the strength of my seventh annual extended residence in the College, I for the seventh time report its expenditures justified by its educational policy.
I hereby certify that the accompanying statements (I. Summary Statement of Income and Expenses, and II. Summary Statement of Financial Condition) are in accordance with its books of account as of the dates indicated, and give, in my judgement, a correct picture of its finances.
(Signed) William Morse Cole

Notes applied to Statement 1.
1In addition to room and board for staff members and their families. This figure is for cash salaries paid; but since the College is owned by the Faculty and financially managed by a committee (on which the student body is represented), the remuneration voted is limited by the funds available after all other expenses are paid. Actually the combined cash and living are well below what faculty members might reasonably expect to receive at institutions of long-established standing, and much less than the amounts originally budgeted on the basis of reasonable needs.
2Both faculty and students wait on themselves and each other, and care for their own rooms.
