MEH: [INSTRUCTIONS NOT TRANSCRIBED] [GIVES IDENTIFICATION] Dorothy,
how did you come to be at Black Mountain College?
DCR: I was a painter and a somewhat serious violinist and wanted a regular
humanities, liberal arts education, and I went to Radcliffe for two years. There
was no studio work at Radcliffe, and it was back in the days, a thousand years
ago, when it was still called Radcliffe. I had to paint and I loved Radcliffe and
yet I was very happy there except for the fact that painting was – there was no
credit for studio work. So I painted half the night. But I would never have left
Radcliffe if I'd been in a Radcliffe sort of field. I was intensely happy there,
academically and with friends, but I heard about Black Mountain. I met a Black
Mountain student, ex-Black Mountain student, an anthropologist, who had
transferred from Black Mountain to Radcliffe for mirror image reasons. She
needed a real anthropology department, which Black Mountain didn't have. She
told me all about it and I knew about Albers. I transferred because I needed to
paint “legally” and I very much wanted to study with Albers. So that's how I got
there.
